      Experiment two · trying to beat 93

      Four reported runs did not beat 93.

      This page reports four runs that started from Bader’s 93-yard construction and tried 1,500 changes each. Most moved one fence. Every 250 tries, a run restarted from Bader’s construction with three random changes. Their recorded summaries report no count above 93. The site includes those summaries, not the per-change logs, and the search explored only drawings close to Bader’s construction.

      How the search chose its moves

      We used Python’s seeded random generator, with seeds 17 and 42 for each of two methods. One changed a fence’s position. The other could change its direction or position. Each change adjusted one whole-number coefficient in the line equation ax + by + c = 0. Its size was the larger of 1 and floor(max(|a|, |b|) / d), with d chosen from 10, 100, 1,000 and 10,000 and either sign chosen at random.

      The recorded method kept ordinary moves when the count stayed level or rose. Every 250 proposals, it restarted from Bader’s construction and applied three changes, accepting a valid restart even if its count fell. Invalid drawings were rejected. Both exact counters had to agree. Each run allowed 1,500 proposals or 120 seconds, whichever came first. All four summaries report that they used their proposal budget. These settings describe the reported 6,000-change experiment, not the earlier search that found 76.

      Rules

      The highlighted rule runs next

      The six-step robot shows a halt with four marked squares. Our bounded run tested all 20,736 two-state rule sets for at most 100 steps, and a third simulator agreed on those bounded outcomes. For 5,912 machines, it found neither a halt nor a repeat proof. They are unresolved by our checker. In this standard blank-tape, two-state, two-symbol model with a left or right move on every step, the standard two-state, two-symbol result S(2) = 6 (also listed by MathWorld). Therefore none of the 5,912 can halt. That conclusion uses the established result, which our bounded run does not prove.

      Reading the evidence

      Each kind of evidence proves one thing.

      01

      Witness

      A checked drawing with 93 yards proves that 93 is possible. It says nothing about whether 94 is.

      02

      Proof

      Proving a maximum would mean showing that no drawing can do better. Checking individual drawings does not settle that question.

      03

      Novelty

      Bader published the 93-yard drawing before we rebuilt it, so our version is a reconstruction of prior art.

      Busy Beaver lesson

      Three outcomes, three meanings

      A halting robot shows that its step count is possible. When it returns to an earlier state with the same 1s at the same distances from it, it will never halt. Reaching step 100 without either result leaves it unresolved by this checker. The standard two-state result settles nonhalting only when applied separately to this exact machine model.
